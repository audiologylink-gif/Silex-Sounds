# Silex Sounds: Nigeria hearing-aid market disruption research

**Decision date:** 4 September 2026
**Scope:** Nigerian hearing-aid hardware, import, service, distribution and affordability—not clinical workflow software.
**Bottom line:** **GO — BUT VALIDATE.** A focused, service-led BTE hearing-aid entrant has a credible pilot opportunity, but the public trade record and the supplied venture brief were not available in this workspace. Silex should not order commercial inventory until it has (1) written NAFDAC classification/registration guidance for the exact device and brand, (2) three Nigerian channel interviews plus 20 price/repair mystery-shops, and (3) sample reliability and spare-parts commitments from two OEMs.

> **Evidence discipline.** “Fact” means supported by a linked primary/credible source in the source register. “Estimate” is an explicitly modelled range. “Inference” is a business conclusion. “Unknown” is intentionally not filled with invented data. The requested `African_Hearing_Aid_Venture_Brief.pdf` was not present in the repository or accessible environment; none of its claims could be verified or compared. Public Nigerian unit-level trade, repair and purchaser data are materially incomplete; estimates below are planning ranges, not market facts.

## 1. Actual Nigerian market

**What the evidence says**

- **FACT:** Hearing aids are classified internationally under HS **9021.40** (“hearing aids, excluding parts and accessories”); parts are separately classified in the 9021 family. Classification must be confirmed against Nigeria’s current tariff/API at entry. [WCO HS overview][S1]
- **FACT:** WHO estimates that more than 80% of people with disabling hearing loss live in low- and middle-income countries and identifies cost and lack of services as access barriers. This supports need, not Nigerian paid demand. [WHO World Report on Hearing][S2]
- **UNKNOWN:** A defensible public series for Nigeria’s HS 9021.40 import *units*, importer names, channel split and public procurement could not be extracted in this environment. Trade databases frequently show customs declarations rather than all informal/courier/cross-border flows; no unit conclusion should be made from value-only data.
- **CONTRADICTION:** The venture brief’s market figures cannot be compared because the PDF was not supplied. Any claim that imports alone represent the market conflicts with the likely presence of donated, traveller-carried, online and bundled clinical sales, but their magnitude is unknown.

**Estimated Nigerian answer (planning triangulation):** 5,000–35,000 devices/year, with **15,000–20,000 base**; annual consumer market **₦1.8bn–₦10.5bn**, base **₦4.0bn–₦5.0bn**. Conservative assumes 5,000 purchasers at ₦350k; base assumes 17,500 at ₦260k average realised price; aggressive assumes 35,000 at ₦300k. This is a *demand-and-channel hypothesis*, not reported import data. Confidence: **Low**.

**What this means for Silex:** It is likely large enough for a 300–1,000 device specialist business, not yet proven large enough to justify a bespoke electronics programme or nationwide fixed clinic network. Buy Comtrade/ITC or a Nigerian customs-data extract by HS 9021.40 and reconcile it to 10 named importers before finalising TAM.

**Decision: GO — BUT VALIDATE.**

Their public corporate materials establish portfolio ownership, not Nigerian authorisation or stock. [Sonova][S3] [Demant][S4] [WSA][S5] [GN][S6]

## 5. Repair and service market

**Known:** Hearing aids are complex medical electronic devices; manufacturer fitting/programming ecosystems and product-specific parts constrain independent repair. Exact Nigerian repair location, turnaround, failure incidence and prices are **UNKNOWN**. Manufacturer product literature confirms rechargeable and wireless systems add device-specific charging/software dependencies. [S3][S5][S6]

**Planning service matrix:**

| Failure / consumable | First-line response | Local stock policy |
|---|---|---|
| weak/no sound, feedback | wax guard/dome/tubing, listening check, reprogramming | 12 months of consumables per installed base |
| receiver fault | swap pre-programmed compatible receiver, verify output | 5–10% of BTE/RIC base only after OEM compatibility proof |
| charging fault | avoid as pilot dependency; swap charger if recharge SKU later | 3–5% charger stock |
| moisture/corrosion | drying, inspection, replacement decision | desiccant + diagnostic workflow; reject unsealed designs |
| microphone/PCB/firmware | controlled OEM RMA or board/module replacement | no board-level repair until manuals, tools and training exist |
| case damage/earmold | replace shell/earhook or local earmold partner | earhooks, battery doors, tubes; documented MOQs |

**Estimated annual parts demand for 1,000 active Silex devices:** 800–1,500 dome/wax/tube sets; 100–250 battery-door/earhook items; 30–100 receivers only if replaceable; 20–60 chargers only if rechargeable; 20–80 out-of-warranty repairs. These are service-planning ranges, not national demand. Assumption: 3–8% annual technical event rate plus consumables. Confidence: Low.

**What this means for Silex:** Local service is a credible advantage only if SKU count is tiny, spares are contractually available and swap/repair times are actually published and achieved. Promise a 7-business-day diagnosis, not an unsupported same-day board repair.

**Decision: GO — BUT VALIDATE.**

## 6. OEM repairability specification (send as RFQ annex)

**Required for pilot SKU:**
1. Adult BTE, 312/13 disposable battery; programmable digitally; no cloud account required for core fitting.
2. Replaceable earhook, battery door, tubing and wax/dome system; OEM must identify compatible part numbers and prices.
3. Standard programming interface and current fitting software licence; offline installer, programming cable, diagnostic/listening procedure and English service manual.
4. Serialised device/lot traceability, 24-month component availability commitment, change-control notice (firmware/hardware/BOM) of at least 90 days.
5. Written failure-code/RMA path; parts MOQ, lead time and warranty terms; 10 sample devices plus service spares.
6. Moisture/sweat-resistance test evidence and stated ingress rating (not marketing claim); corrosion-resistant battery contacts; drying/storage instructions.
7. 12-month replacement warranty from Silex receipt; OEM replenishment/credit rules; dead-on-arrival limit and acceptance sampling agreed.
8. No app, Bluetooth, rechargeable pack, custom acoustics, shell/tooling, firmware or acoustic-gain changes in v1 unless tested and included in the technical file.

**Preferred (not gate):** replaceable receiver, sealed microphone module, conformal coating declaration, locally trainable Level-1 service, multilingual packaging artwork control. **Do not demand IP68 without a test standard/report; it may add cost without fixing the dominant failures.**

**What this means for Silex:** The required documentation and service access are more valuable than a superficial OEM logo/colour change.

**Decision: GO.**

## 10. Certification and private-label risk

**FACT:** CE marking and FDA pathways are product/manufacturer-specific regulatory constructs; ISO 13485 alone is not device approval. [S7][S8][S9] **Inference:** A Silex logo/packaging can make Silex an importer, distributor, specification developer or legal manufacturer depending on the changes and regulatory framework. It cannot be assumed that an OEM certificate automatically transfers.

**Safest v1 customisation:** brand artwork/IFU only, after NAFDAC and OEM written approval; preserve legal manufacturer, exact model, intended use, acoustics, BOM, firmware, charger and software. **Do not modify:** gain algorithm, firmware, app, battery, enclosure, receiver, microphone, acoustic coupling, ingress claim or medical claims. Maintain a documented private-label agreement, declaration/certificate/model mapping and change notification.

**What this means for Silex:** No tooling, custom Bluetooth app or African “rugged” redesign in the first registration.

**Decision: GO — BUT VALIDATE.**

# Final Silex Sounds decision memo

1. **Market opportunity:** 5,000–35,000 devices/year; base 15,000–20,000. **Low confidence; buy trade data.**
2. **Current consumer price:** unverified public national range; planning comparator ₦350k–₦1m+ for fitted premium, but must mystery-shop.
3. **Estimated landed cost:** ₦175k–₦365k fully loaded variable cost in first batch; lower only with validated volume and tariff treatment.
4. **Silex target retail price:** ₦250k cash only if variable cost ≤₦210k; otherwise ₦300k–₦350k fitted.
5. **Estimated gross margin:** 17–37% at ₦350k on the base cost model; 3% at ₦300k; negative at ₦250k if costs are not beaten down.
6. **First product:** one digitally programmable adult disposable-battery BTE platform.
7. **Second product:** power BTE only after measured referral demand; no third launch SKU.
8. **Primary distribution channel:** three Lagos/Abuja ENT/audiology referral/fitting partners plus Silex-owned service/lead funnel.
9. **Repair model:** local Level-1 triage/consumables/swaps + controlled OEM RMA for electronics.
10. **Spare-parts model:** initial 15% service spares plus 12-month consumables, contracted 24-month availability.
11. **OEM top 3 for verified RFQ:** Audina, Earsmate, NewSound (provisional; no certification endorsement).
12. **Regulatory budget:** ₦6m–₦15m realistic, plus contingency; confirm directly with NAFDAC/local counsel.
13. **Initial capital requirement:** ₦65m–₦95m for a 300-unit supported pilot.
14. **First-year unit target:** 300 units ordered only after gates; 180 sell-through within 120 days of receipt.
15. **Biggest risk:** FX- and channel-driven inability to sell at a positive contribution price.
16. **Biggest opportunity:** replace opaque, slow service with visible local stock, serialised warranty and repair turnaround.
17. **Competitive moat:** partner network + parts/service data + availability + regulated brand trust.
18. **GO / NO-GO:** **GO — BUT VALIDATE.** Proceed to a 10–20-device sample and 300-unit gated pilot, not a scale launch.
